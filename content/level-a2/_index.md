## What A2 covers

A2 is the level of routine transactions and simple description of the past and
the immediate environment. The centre of gravity of the level is the past —
Portuguese distinguishes the *pretérito perfeito simples* from the *imperfeito*
along a boundary that does not map onto the German *Perfekt* / *Präteritum*
split, and getting that boundary right is most of what A2 is for.

- **Grammar.** *Pretérito perfeito simples* (regular and the irregular core:
  *ser/ir*, *ter*, *estar*, *fazer*, *poder*, *dizer*, *ver*, *vir*);
  *imperfeito* and its aspectual value; the future with *ir* and the simple
  future; direct and indirect object pronouns and their placement; comparatives
  and superlatives; the affirmative and negative imperative; *por* and *para*;
  relative clauses with *que* and *onde*.
- **Function.** Narrating a trip, describing an illness, reporting a problem
  with a flat, writing a short CV and a covering message, comparing two options,
  giving instructions and a recipe, expressing a simple opinion about something
  read or watched.
- **Register.** The formal / informal split now extends to writing: an email to
  a landlord is not an email to a friend. Formulae of opening and closing
  (*Exmo. Senhor*, *Com os melhores cumprimentos*) are taught explicitly.
- **Text length.** Reading texts move from 60–90 words at A1 to 150–220 words;
  writing tasks from a message to a structured paragraph of 80–120 words.

## The twelve units

| № | Unit | Focus | Skill emphasis |
|--:|------|-------|----------------|
|  1 | Ontem e antigamente — perfeito e imperfeito | The aspect contrast, time adverbials | Language awareness |
|  2 | Viagens e transportes | Travel narration, booking, timetables | Listening |

|  7 | Comparar e escolher | Comparatives, superlatives, argument for a choice | Reading |

## Exam target

As at A1, each unit has a sibling exam bundle sharing its unit number, with the
printable PDF under `static/downloads/a2/`. A2 exams add a longer reading text
and a structured writing task with a marking grid, and the speaking component
moves from a scripted exchange to a semi-guided one.

## No Brasil

At A2 the contrast boxes shift from vocabulary to structure: the Brazilian
preference for the *pretérito perfeito composto* in some contexts where Portugal
uses the simple past; proclisis where European Portuguese is enclitic
(*me diga* / *diga-me*); *a gente* with third-person singular agreement; and
the near-total loss of *tu* verb morphology in most Brazilian registers even
where the pronoun survives.
