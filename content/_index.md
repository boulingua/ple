## What this site is

This is the Portuguese arm of **boulingua**, a family of openly-licensed
language courses by S. Le Boulanger. It is built for the German
*Gesamtschule* system and for independent adult learners, and it is free in
both senses: no account, no paywall, and a licence that permits reuse and
adaptation with attribution.

The course is organised as four stages — a short pre-A1 stage on sounds and
spelling, then CEFR **A1**, **A2** and **B1**. Each level is a run of roughly
twelve units. Every unit follows the same five-move shape, is paired with a
model exam, and ships with a slide deck, a printable worksheet, an answer key
and native-voice audio.

## The five-move unit

Each unit runs the same didactic sequence, so that it is plannable for the
teacher and predictable for the learner:

1. **Activate** (*Activar*) — recover prior knowledge; a picture prompt, a
   question, a two-minute brainstorm.
2. **Input** (*Entrada*) — the model dialogue or text, the vocabulary, the
   grammar point. Always originally written for this course.
3. **Practise** (*Praticar*) — guided, closed-form rehearsal of the target
   language, with the key in a collapsible block.
4. **Apply** (*Aplicar*) — the free-production task: writing, speaking,
   role-play, mediation. This is what differentiation scaffolds.
5. **Reflect** (*Reflectir*) — a metacognitive close; the learner checks
   themselves against the unit's can-do statements.

Exam units shift the sequence to **task → model → strategy → attempt →
feedback**.

## European Portuguese, with Brazil in view

The single decision that shapes everything else is which norm to teach. This
course teaches **European Portuguese**: the pronunciation, the pronoun system,
the vocabulary and the orthography of Portugal, post-1990. The reason is
destination realism — the learners this course is written for are in Europe,
and the sister courses serve the same school context.

That choice is not a claim that European Portuguese is the more important
variety; roughly four in five speakers of Portuguese are Brazilian. It is a
decision to teach **one** consistent norm rather than an average of two, and
then to say plainly where the other differs. Every unit therefore carries a
compact **No Brasil** box recording the concrete deltas — *tu* versus *você*,
gerund versus *a* + infinitive, *comboio* versus *trem*, the vowel reductions
that Brazilian Portuguese does not make. See [About this course](/about/) for
the full rationale and the style rules that follow from it.

## Materials

Slide decks (editable **.odp** plus PDF) and printable **PDF** worksheets are
generated from the branded LaTeX templates and committed to the repository, so
they are versioned alongside the prose rather than regenerated on every build.
Audio is produced with a native European Portuguese voice through the shared
`audiogen` workflow. See [Materials](/materials/).

## Status

The site is live and correctly branded; unit content is being written level by
level. Pre-A1 and A1 come first — that is the point at which the course goes
from a scaffold to something a teacher can take into a classroom. Progress and
sequencing are recorded in
[`ROADMAP.md`](https://github.com/boulingua/ple/blob/main/ROADMAP.md).
