## What B1 covers

B1 is the threshold of independence: the learner can deal with most situations
that arise while travelling, can produce connected text on familiar topics, and
can describe experiences, opinions and plans with reasons. In Portuguese the
grammatical price of admission is the **conjuntivo**, and B1 is where the course
pays it — introduced through its most frequent, least abstract environments
(*é preciso que*, *espero que*, *talvez*, *quando* + future subjunctive) rather
than as a paradigm to be memorised.

- **Grammar.** Present, imperfect and future subjunctive in their high-frequency
  triggers; the conditional and the *se* clause types; the personal infinitive —
  a Portuguese speciality with no German counterpart; passive and impersonal
  constructions; reported speech; discourse connectors of concession, cause and
  consequence.
- **Function.** Stating and defending an opinion, conceding and rebutting,
  complaining formally, writing a letter of application and a letter of
  complaint, summarising an article, chairing a short discussion, taking part in
  an online thread.
- **Mediation.** B1 is the first level at which the CEFR mediation scales carry
  usable descriptors, so the course implements them rather than declaring them
  empty: relaying specific information, processing a text in speech and in
  writing, and facilitating a simple exchange between a Portuguese speaker and a
  German speaker.
- **Text length.** Reading texts of 300–450 words, including one authentic-shaped
  journalistic register; writing tasks of 150–200 words with a stated structure.

|  1 | Na minha opinião — tomar posição | Opinion markers, connectors, structured argument | Speaking (production) |

## Exam target

Eleven sibling exam bundles, PDFs under `static/downloads/b1/`. B1 exams are
modular in shape — reading, listening, writing, speaking scored separately — and
add a mediation task, since mediation is assessable at this level and is a
declared part of the course's conformance claim.

## The Lusophone world

B1 is where the course stops being a course about Portugal. Unit 9 treats the
Portuguese-speaking world as a linguistic and cultural fact rather than an
appendix: nine countries, four continents, and a language whose centre of
demographic gravity is in the southern hemisphere. The *No Brasil* boxes remain,
but at B1 they are joined by short notes on African and Asian varieties where a
text or a recording makes them relevant.

## Beyond B1

**B2** is declared as a stretch phase, not a launch requirement, and the course
states plainly that it does not meet `full` conformance. That is deliberate: it
is more useful to ship a complete, well-made A1–B1 than a thin A1–C1. Should B2
be written, it is recorded in `ROADMAP.md` as milestone M6, alongside a possible
Brazilian-Portuguese track branched from the same source.
