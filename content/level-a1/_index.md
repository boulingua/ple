## What A1 covers

A1 in the CEFR is the level at which a learner can introduce themselves, ask
and answer simple questions about immediate needs, and interact in a simple way
provided the other person speaks slowly and is willing to help. Concretely, in
this course, that means twelve units built on:

- **Grammar.** The present indicative of regular *-ar / -er / -ir* verbs and
  the high-frequency irregulars (*ser*, *estar*, *ter*, *ir*, *fazer*, *poder*,
  *querer*); gender and number; articles and contractions (*do*, *na*, *pelo*);
  possessives; the *ir* + infinitive near future; and, at the end of the level,
  a first controlled exposure to the *pretérito perfeito simples*.
- **Function.** Greeting, introducing, spelling aloud, asking prices, ordering,
  asking the way, telling the time, arranging to meet, describing a room, and
  writing a short message.
- **Register.** *Ser* versus *estar* is taught early, but the harder contrast at
  A1 is social: *tu* with peers, *você* with distance, *o senhor* / *a senhora*
  with deference — and the Portuguese habit of avoiding the pronoun altogether
  by using the third person with a title. This is introduced in unit 1 and
  revisited in every unit that involves a service encounter.
- **Pronunciation.** A recurring short *Pronúncia* note in each unit,
  continuing the work of [Pre-A1](/pre-a1/).

## The recurring cast

One small ensemble runs through all twelve units, so that vocabulary,
relationships and situations compound rather than resetting each week:

- **Rita Marques** — thirty-four, from Lisboa. Works in a bookshop in Alcântara.
  The anchor character: patient, direct, speaks at a normal pace.
- **Tomás** — nineteen, Rita's neighbour's son, a first-year student at a Lisbon
  university. Chronically late, useful for timetables and routine.
- **Dona Amélia** — seventy-one, the neighbour on the first floor. The reason
  the course can teach *a senhora*, formal address and the third-person
  politeness pattern without inventing a contrived situation.
- **Lena** — an Erasmus student from Germany, in Lisbon for two semesters. The
  learner's proxy: she asks the questions a German-speaking beginner asks, and
  she makes the mistakes a German-speaking beginner makes.

## Exam target

Each unit has a **sibling exam bundle** sharing the unit's number: a
first-class page in its own right, with a printable PDF under
`static/downloads/a1/`. The exams follow a stable A1 shape — reading, listening,
writing and a paired speaking task — with a stated point total and a
*Notenschlüssel*, so a teacher can use one as a class test without adaptation.
Every stimulus text is written for this course; no published exam material is
reproduced.

## Curriculum alignment

Every unit's front matter carries a `curriculum` block against the CEFR
framework, and every can-do claim resolves to a framework descriptor ID of the
form `A1.INT.information-exchange.01`. Where a CEFR scale has no descriptor at
A1 — much of mediation, most of the plurilingual scales — the course records
`no-official-descriptor` rather than inventing coverage. The build fails on any
identifier that does not resolve.

## No Brasil

Every unit carries a compact contrast box. At A1 the recurring items are: *você*
as the ordinary second person in most of Brazil (against *tu* in Portugal);
*a gente* for *nós*; the gerund (*estou falando*) where European Portuguese uses
*estar a* + infinitive; proclitic pronoun placement; and the everyday lexical
splits — *comboio* / *trem*, *autocarro* / *ônibus*, *pequeno-almoço* /
*café da manhã*, *casa de banho* / *banheiro*.
